**A Shakshuka Recipe That Makes Breakfast Feel Like Dinner**

There’s something magical about a skillet of simmering tomatoes, fragrant spices, and eggs poached right in the sauce—it feels like a lazy weekend brunch, yet it’s hearty enough to satisfy a dinner craving. I first discovered shakshuka on a rainy Tuesday when I wanted something quick, colorful, and a little exotic without turning on the oven. The dish came together in under 30 minutes, smelled like a North African market, and left me reaching for crusty bread to sop up every last drop. If you’ve ever wished breakfast could double as a cozy dinner, this is the recipe that delivers.

## Why It Matters

Shakshuka has become a staple on menus from Tel Aviv cafés to Brooklyn brunch spots because it’s adaptable, nutritious, and deeply comforting. The base—tomatoes, peppers, onions, and garlic—provides vitamins and antioxidants, while the eggs add protein and richness. Because the dish is cooked in one pan, cleanup is minimal, making it ideal for busy weeknights or leisurely weekends. Moreover, the spice level can be tweaked to suit any palate, and the final garnish of feta or herbs adds a bright, salty finish that elevates the humble ingredients into something special. In short, shakshuka proves that a simple pantry meal can feel both familiar and exciting.

## The Sauce Base

Both Smitten Kitchen and Love and Lemons start with a classic sofrito: olive oil, diced onion, and bell pepper softened until translucent, followed by garlic. The Smitten Kitchen version uses **1 Tbsp olive oil**, **1 small onion**, and **1 red bell pepper**, while Love and Lemons calls for **2 Tbsp olive oil**, **1 onion**, and **1 bell pepper** (any color works). After the vegetables are tender, both recipes add minced garlic (2 cloves in Smitten Kitchen, 3 in Love and Lemons) and cook it briefly to avoid bitterness.

> **💡 Pro Tip:** If you like a deeper, sweeter sauce, add a teaspoon of tomato paste (as Love and Lemons does) and let it caramelize for a minute before adding the tomatoes.

## Spicing It Up

The spice blend is where the two sources diverge slightly, giving you room to tailor the heat. Smitten Kitchen recommends **1 tsp ground cumin**, **1 tsp smoked paprika**, and a **pinch of cayenne** (optional). Love and Lemons uses the same cumin and smoked paprika amounts but swaps cayenne for **¼ tsp red pepper flakes** and sometimes adds a splash of harissa for extra depth. Both agree that the spices should be toasted in the oil for about 30 seconds—this releases their aromatics without burning.

> **⚠️ Watch Out:** Over‑toasting the spices can make them bitter; keep the heat medium and stir constantly.

Once the spices are fragrant, add the canned tomatoes. Smitten Kitchen uses a **28‑oz can of crushed tomatoes**; Love and Lemons opts for a **28‑oz can of diced tomatoes** plus **2 Tbsp tomato paste**. Both recipes call for seasoning with **salt and pepper** to taste, and Smitten Kitchen adds a **tiny pinch of sugar** to balance acidity—a step Love and Lemons omits but notes you can add if the tomatoes taste sharp.

> **🎯 Key Insight:** Simmer the sauce uncovered for **10‑12 minutes** (Smitten Kitchen) or **8‑10 minutes** (Love and Lemons) until it thickens slightly and the flavors meld. This step is crucial; rushing it yields a watery shakshuka.

## Eggs & Finish

After the sauce has reduced, make small wells with a spoon and crack the eggs directly into them. Both sources suggest **4‑6 large eggs**, depending on pan size and how hungry you are. Cover the skillet and let the eggs poach until the whites are set but the yolks remain runny—**about 5‑8 minutes**. Check frequently; over‑cooked yolks lose the luscious sauce‑soaking quality that makes the dish special.

Finish with a crumble of **feta cheese** (≈¼ cup) and a sprinkle of fresh **parsley or cilantro** (≈2 Tbsp chopped). Love and Lemons also suggests a drizzle of extra‑virgin olive oil or a few drops of lemon juice for brightness.

> **💡 Pro Tip:** If you prefer your eggs firmer, cover the pan for an extra minute or two; for ultra‑runny yolks, keep the lid off and baste the tops with hot sauce.

## Practical Details

### Ingredients (combined from both sources)

- Olive oil: 1‑2 Tbsp  
- Onion: 1 small (or ½ large), diced  
- Bell pepper: 1 (any color), diced  
- Garlic: 2‑3 cloves, minced  
- Ground cumin: 1 tsp  
- Smoked paprika: 1 tsp  
- Cayenne pepper **or** red pepper flakes: pinch to ¼ tsp (adjust to heat preference)  
- Crushed tomatoes: 1 × 28‑oz can (or diced tomatoes + 2 Tbsp tomato paste)  
- Salt & freshly ground black pepper: to taste  
- Sugar (optional): a tiny pinch (Smitten Kitchen)  
- Eggs: 4‑6 large  
- Feta cheese: ¼ cup, crumbled  
- Fresh parsley or cilantro: 2 Tbsp, chopped  
- Optional garnish: extra‑virgin olive oil, lemon juice, harissa

### Steps

1. **Heat oil** in a large cast‑iron or nonstick skillet over medium heat.  
2. **Sauté onion and bell pepper** until softened, about 5 minutes.  
3. **Add garlic** and cook 30 seconds more, stirring constantly.  
4. **Stir in cumin, smoked paprika, and cayenne/red‑pepper flakes**; toast 30 seconds until fragrant.  
5. **Pour in tomatoes** (and tomato paste if using), season with salt, pepper, and a pinch of sugar if desired.  
6. **Simmer uncovered** 8‑12 minutes, stirring occasionally, until sauce thickens.  
7. **Create wells** in the sauce and crack eggs into them.  
8. **Cover skillet** and cook 5‑8 minutes, or until egg whites are set but yolks remain runny.  
9. **Remove from heat**, scatter feta and herbs over the top, drizzle with olive oil or lemon juice if using.  
10. **Serve immediately** with crusty bread, pita, or warm tortillas for dipping.

### Substitutions & Variations

- **Vegetables:** Add sliced mushrooms, spinach, or zucchini with the onions for extra veggies.  
- **Protein:** Stir in cooked chickpeas or sliced merguez sausage for a heartier version.  
- **Cheese:** Swap feta for goat cheese, cotija, or a sprinkle of parmesan.  
- **Heat:** Increase cayenne or add a teaspoon of harissa for more kick; reduce or omit for a milder dish.  
- **Tomatoes:** Fresh ripe tomatoes can be used (about 1 ½ lb, peeled and chopped) but will require a longer simmer to break down.  
- **Egg‑free:** For a vegan take, replace eggs with silken tofu cubes or cooked lentils and increase the sauce volume.

### Serving Ideas

- **Classic:** Scoop shakshuka onto plates with warm, crusty bread or toasted pita on the side.  
- **Brunch board:** Pair with a simple green salad, sliced avocado, and a dollop of labneh or yogurt.  
- **Dinner twist:** Serve over a bed of couscous, quinoa, or roasted sweet potatoes for a more substantial meal.  
- **Leftover love:** The sauce reheats beautifully; top with a freshly fried egg the next day for a quick breakfast‑for‑dinner.

## Key Takeaways

- Shakshuka’s core—tomatoes, peppers, onions, garlic, spices, and eggs—is consistent across trusted recipes; the differences lie in optional tomato paste, sugar, and heat level.  
- Simmering the sauce before adding eggs ensures a rich, thick base that clings to the poached eggs.  
- The dish is endlessly adaptable: swap veggies, adjust spice, change the cheese, or serve it over grains for dinner‑worthy comfort.  
- One‑pan cooking means minimal cleanup, making it ideal for both rushed weeknights and leisurely weekends.  
- Finish with fresh herbs and a quality olive oil (or a squeeze of lemon) to brighten the deep, smoky flavors.

**The Homemade Pizza Night Recipe That Feels Worth It**

There’s something magical about pulling a steaming, cheese‑laden pan pizza out of the oven on a weeknight—especially when the crust is crisp on the outside, tender inside, and the toppings are exactly what you’re craving. I’ve tried a dozen “quick pizza” tricks, but the versions that consistently deliver that restaurant‑worthy feel are the pan‑pizza methods from King Arthur Baking and Serious Eats. Below I break down what they agree on, where they diverge, and how you can turn their guidance into a foolproof, satisfying pizza night at home.

## Why It Matters

Pan‑style pizza sits between the thin‑crisp New York slice and the deep‑dish Chicago pie, offering a satisfying bite that’s sturdy enough for generous toppings yet still light enough to enjoy several slices. For home cooks, the pan format removes the need for a pizza stone or peel, and the high‑edge crust traps sauce and cheese, reducing messy slides. Mastering a reliable pan‑pizza recipe means you can whip up a crowd‑pleasing dinner with minimal fuss—perfect for game night, a casual date, or a solo treat after work.

## The Pan‑Pizza Philosophy

Both sources start with the same core idea: a **wet, high‑hydration dough** baked in a well‑oiled pan creates a crust that’s simultaneously crispy on the bottom and chewy inside. The pan acts like a mini‑steam oven, trapping moisture while the hot oven browns the exterior.  

> **💡 Pro Tip:** Lightly coat the pan with olive oil (or a mix of oil and butter) before adding the dough. This not only prevents sticking but also fries the edge of the crust for extra flavor and crunch.

Where they differ is in the **handling of the dough** and the **cheese strategy**:

- **King Arthur’s Crispy Cheesy Pan Pizza** leans into a generous layer of shredded mozzarella mixed with a bit of Parmesan, then finishes with a sprinkle of garlic‑herb butter after baking for an aromatic punch.  
- **Serious Eats’ Foolproof Pan Pizza** recommends a more modest cheese blanket (mostly low‑moisture mozzarella) and suggests adding toppings *before* the cheese so they bake into the sauce, creating a cohesive layer.

Understanding these nuances lets you tailor the pie to your mood—extra indulgent or balanced and bright.

## Practical Details

Below is a distilled, step‑by‑step guide that honors the points of agreement between the two recipes while offering flexible options for ingredients, substitutions, and serving ideas.

### Ingredients (makes one 10‑inch pan pizza; scale up as needed)

| Component | What the sources agree on | Optional tweaks |
|-----------|---------------------------|-----------------|
| **Flour** | Bread flour or high‑gluten flour for structure (both recipes call for it) | All‑purpose works in a pinch; expect a slightly softer crust |
| **Water** | Cold or room‑temp water, enough to reach a ~70 % hydration dough (wet but manageable) | Adjust by a tablespoon if dough feels too dry or sticky |
| **Yeast** | Instant or active dry yeast (both recipes use a small amount for slow rise) | You can use a sourdough starter discard (≈¼ cup) and reduce yeast accordingly |
| **Salt** | Fine sea salt, essential for flavor and gluten tightening | — |
| **Olive oil** | Added to the dough (≈1‑2 Tbsp) and to the pan for frying the crust | Infused oils (garlic, chili) add extra depth |
| **Sauce** | Simple tomato sauce (crushed tomatoes, a pinch of salt, optional oregano) | Use store‑bought marinara, pesto, or a white garlic‑olive oil base |
| **Cheese** | Low‑moisture mozzarella shredded (both); King Arthur adds Parmesan; Serious Eats keeps it mozzarella‑only | Try provolone, fontina, or a sprinkle of smoked gouda for variety |
| **Toppings** | Any pre‑cooked or quick‑cooking items (pepperoni, sautéed veggies, cooked sausage) | Fresh basil, arugula, or a drizzle of hot honey after baking |

### Steps

1. **Make the dough**  
   - In a bowl, combine flour, yeast, and salt. Add water and olive oil; stir until a shaggy ball forms.  
   - Knead (by hand 6‑8 min or mixer 4‑5 min) until smooth and elastic. The dough should be tacky but not stick excessively to your hands.  
   - Cover and let rise at room temperature 1‑2 h, or refrigerate overnight for deeper flavor. Both sources note that a cold ferment improves texture but is optional.

2. **Prepare the pan**  
   - Generously coat a 10‑inch cast‑iron skillet or heavy non‑stick pan with olive oil (about 1‑2 Tbsp).  
   - If using butter for extra flavor, melt a teaspoon in the pan before adding oil.

3. **Shape & proof**  
   - Transfer the risen dough to the oiled pan. Using oiled fingertips, gently press it out to the edges, leaving a slight lip for the crust.  
   - Let the dough rest 15‑20 min (this relaxes gluten and prevents shrinkage).

4. **Add sauce & toppings**  
   - Spread a thin layer of sauce (≈¼‑½ cup) over the dough, leaving a border.  
   - Distribute toppings evenly. **Serious Eats** suggests placing toppings *under* the cheese for better integration; **King Arthur** adds cheese first, then toppings. Choose whichever you prefer—both work.

5. **Cheese it up**  
   - Sprinkle the shredded mozzarella (and Parmesan if using King Arthur’s style) over the top.  
   - For a richer finish, drizzle a little garlic‑herb butter (melted butter + minced garlic + parsley) after baking (King Arthur) or before baking for a golden crust (Serious Eats).

6. **Bake**  
   - Preheat oven to a high temperature (both recipes call for a hot oven—typically 425‑475 °F).  
   - Bake 12‑18 minutes, until the edges are deep golden‑brown and the cheese is bubbling.  
   - If you like extra crispness, switch to broil for the last 1‑2 minutes—watch closely!

7. **Rest & serve**  
   - Let the pizza sit 2‑3 minutes before slicing; this lets the cheese set and prevents a soggy center.  
   - Slice, serve hot, and enjoy the contrast of crisp edge, tender interior, and melty topping.

### Substitutions & Ideas

- **Gluten‑free:** Use a 1‑to‑1 gluten‑free bread‑flour blend; increase water slightly as GF flours absorb more liquid. Expect a less chewy but still crisp crust.  
- **Vegan:** Omit dairy cheese; use a melty vegan mozzarella or a blend of cashew‑based cheese and nutritional yeast. Add a splash of olive oil to the pan for extra browning.  
- **Low‑sodium:** Reduce added salt in the dough to ½ tsp and choose a no‑salt‑added tomato sauce.  
- **Herb‑infused crust:** Mix dried oregano, basil, or crushed red pepper into the olive oil before coating the pan.  
- **Sweet finish:** After baking, drizzle with a thin layer of honey or balsamic reduction and top with fresh basil or arugula for a sweet‑savory twist.

### Serving Ideas

- Pair with a simple arugula salad dressed with lemon vinaigrette to cut through the richness.  
- Serve alongside garlic knots or rosemary focaccia for a carb‑lover’s spread.  
- For a crowd, double the recipe and bake two pans side‑by‑side; keep the first warm under foil while the second finishes.  
- Leftovers reheat beautifully in a hot skillet (cover with a lid for a minute to revive meltiness) or in a 350 °F oven for 5‑7 minutes.

## Key Takeaways

- **Wet dough + oiled pan = crispy‑chewy crust** – the core technique both sources champion.  
- **High oven temperature** is non‑negotiable for that golden edge; exact temps can vary by oven, so watch for color rather than relying solely on the clock.  
- **Cheese and topping order** is flexible: Serious Eats puts toppings under cheese for integration; King Arthur adds cheese first for a melt‑topped finish. Choose based on texture preference.  
- **Resting the dough** (even a brief 15‑minute bench rest) prevents shrinkage and makes shaping easier.  
- **Finish with butter or herbs** (garlic‑herb butter, a drizzle of olive oil, or fresh basil) adds a restaurant‑level pop right before serving.  
- **Substitutions work**—gluten‑free, vegan, or low‑sodium tweaks are possible; just keep the wet‑dough, oiled‑pan foundation intact.
